A layered lesson on our own pipeline

Our data pipeline

How a course (scanned book, slides, homework, lecture videos), eight textbooks and two libraries become passages a retriever can search and a knowledge graph a tutor can walk. Twenty stages. Click any stage for what it does, why we chose it, what we rejected, the real numbers and what is lost.

TL;DR

Two branches from one passage store

  1. Text first. Scans and maths-heavy pages go through OCR (MinerU2.5-Pro on free remote GPUs); clean text layers are read directly; lectures use their caption tracks. done: 1,075 course pages and 5,694 textbook pages, with 0 failed units.
  2. Structure next. Every book has a table-of-contents tree (chapter, section, printed pages). Rules clean the text and cut one unit per section, children above 1,024 tokens. done: 8,515 searchable units over nine sources, exercises and solutions kept apart.
  3. Search branch. Each unit has a bge-m3 dense and sparse vector. done. The tutor app searches them with pgvector, BM25 and RRF app. The thesis arms read a hashed snapshot; the text arm R was tuned on the development split (30 passages from each list, the top 10 reordered by a cross-encoder), and every cross-encoder score comes from one scorer, set before tuning (197,073 scores). done
  4. Graph branch. Claude Opus 5.5 extracted 10,246 typed edges from 8,069 prose units (the graph's own unit set; the search store holds 8,515 units), entity resolution merged the names, two checkers scored 7,661 evidence pairs on the university GPU, and an Opus prerequisite judge, then two blind re-judges, settled the 1,992 prerequisite edges (331 dropped, 66 restored). done. The graph is frozen under one snapshot hash SG 7f28a3a3 with 5,866 eligible edges. Neo4j serves the tutor app (its reloaded export matches the hash); the thesis arms read the snapshot files.
  5. Two libraries, same steps. The Zotero library (5,186 + 121 papers, 585,574 vectors) and 37 learning books went through the same OCR, units and vectors. done
  6. Where it runs. The Mac only orchestrates. OCR, embedding and the checkers run on Kaggle, Colab and the university's A100 slice; a ledger makes every unit run once. What happens at question time is the companion page, What happens when a student asks, with one turn as built in the app and saying "I don't know".
Level 0in one minute

A library that builds its own card catalogue

For anyone. No technical words.

A librarian receives a box of old photocopied books and a pile of lecture recordings. She wants two things: a card index where any sentence can be found, and a wall map that shows which idea depends on which.

1 · Read

Photocopies cannot be searched, so a fast reader types them out, page by page, and writes the page number on each sheet.

2 · Sort

She files the sheets by chapter and section, using the book's own contents page.

3 · Cards

She cuts each section into cards small enough to read at once. Each card keeps its book, section and page.

4 · Two indexes

One drawer is sorted by words; another is sorted by meaning, so "wide gap" finds "large margin".

5 · The map

A helper reads each card and draws arrows on the wall map: "soft margin uses slack variables". Each arrow points to its card.

6 · Check

Two proof-readers check every arrow against its card. Doubtful arrows wait for the librarian.

The fast reader is the OCR model. The two drawers are BM25 and the vector index. The wall map is the knowledge graph. The proof-readers are the entailment checkers.

    The full record and the evidence behind it

    Each stage below gives the tool, the reason, what we rejected and the evidence label. Labels: count = counted from a named file; measured = a timing or score from our own run; model assessment = an LLM's verdict; inferred = our arithmetic; external = reported by others, not reproduced.

    What the outside evidence says, and how far it carries

    ExternalOn the OmniDocBench leaderboard, MinerU2.5-Pro scores 95.75 overall; its own paper reports 95.69 and a formula score (CDM) of 97.29 (Table 2). The gap between the two is not explained. Several other models score higher (PaddleOCR-VL-1.6: 96.34). Benchmarks decided the shortlist; our own 14-page pilot decided the model. [MinerU2.5-Pro paper; OmniDocBench README]
    External, unverifiedOn the arXiv subset of the READoc converter benchmark, markitdown scored 35.2 against 73.1 for the MinerU pipeline (Table 3; we saw the numbers, not the full paper). An external subset result on other documents: it motivates our choice and a test, not a measured gain on our corpus. [READoc]
    External, provisionalStructure-aware chunking helped in small or single-domain studies, not on text without real hierarchy (papers not read in full). No rule wins everywhere. Our own six-rule pilot was inconclusive. So "section first" is a testable default, and four tests on the development split come before it is fixed.
    DocumentationPostgreSQL's ts_rank does not use corpus-wide term statistics, so it is not BM25; pgvector supports half-precision vectors and an exact scan or HNSW. Reciprocal Rank Fusion scores a passage by the sum of 1/(k + rank) over the lists; its original paper used k = 60, which is not shown to be best for our corpus. [PostgreSQL docs; pgvector README; Cormack et al.]
    Ours, measuredbge-m3: macro recall@10 0.474 against Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B 0.444 and EmbeddingGemma 2 (768-d) 0.353, on the 32 of 40 homework queries with a top-grade passage in the judged pool, over transcript chunks, one LLM judge, paired bootstrap; bge-m3 vs Qwen3 is not resolved. EmbeddingGemma 2 is 12.0 points lower (95% interval −24.9 to −0.3). This is a pilot: no book prose, no formulas, no Persian.
    Ours, measuredEntailment checkers on long premises (76 pairs of 925-1,318 tokens, partly constructed): MiniCheck reached balanced accuracy 0.732 on the whole premise and 0.454 and 0.501 under two ways of truncating it at 512 tokens. Labels are by an LLM. Provisional rule: score the whole premise within MiniCheck's 2,048-token cap, never truncate at 512. The 0.5 threshold is the checkers' own; no labelled set of our pairs exists to calibrate it, so in the thesis the checkers stay secondary and descriptive.
