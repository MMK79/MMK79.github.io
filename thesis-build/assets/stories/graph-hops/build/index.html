A layered lesson on multi-hop retrieval

Graph hops and ranking

Every claim carries a mark from its source: verified unverified inferred ours.

We built the knowledge graph so the tutor can reason across concepts: from the idea a student names to the ideas it rests on. This page shows where that chain broke in our pipeline, and how we fixed it. The walk was fine. The ranking threw the far hops away.

TL;DR

A budget problem, not a depth problem

  1. The graph walk reaches concepts one and two steps away (hops 1 and 2).
  2. The old rank rule (rule 7) sorts by hop first and keeps 10 passages. One seed concept has 38 or more passages, so all 10 slots go to hop 0.
  3. A deeper walk cannot fix this, because the cut happens after the walk.
  4. The fix (Addendum D) shares the 10 slots between the hops: 4 / 3 / 3 when every hop has enough passages.
  5. Most published systems fix their depth in advance; others use a soft score or let an LLM decide when to stop. The thesis keeps a fixed depth of 2 and the fixed quota.
  6. The walk is a BFS so that the edge labels are the only difference between the arms. PPR has the better multi-hop record; communities suit only global questions.
Level 0in one minute

A bag with ten pockets

For anyone. No technical words.

The map

A map of ideas has arrows. "Kernel trick" points to "Mercer kernel". "Mercer kernel" points to "positive definite matrix".

The shelves

Each idea has a shelf of notes. The first shelf is the idea in the question. The next shelves are one and two arrows away.

The bag

You may carry only 10 notes to the person who answers.

The old rule

"Fill the bag from the nearest shelf first." The nearest shelf has 38 notes. The bag is full before you reach the second shelf.

The problem

The answer needs a note from the far shelf. It never gets into the bag.

The new rule

Give each shelf its own pockets: 4 for the near shelf, 3 for the next, 3 for the far one. Take the best notes from each.

Level 1for a student

Seven steps from question to context

Terms are defined on first use. The pipeline runs once for each question; nothing is learned at question time. The knowledge graph (KG) is built offline and frozen. How the KG is built · The whole question path, arm by arm

link→route→walk (BFS)→CE score→rank→RRF→pack

  1. Link: find the seed concepts

    The code finds concept names and synonyms in the question text. It also takes up to 3 concept labels whose embedding is close to the question (similarity 0.60 or more). These concepts are the seeds. They are hop 0.

  2. Route: choose the relations

    A fixed table of text cues ("prove", "why does", "prerequisite") chooses a relation mask (which edge types the walk may follow) and one direction. No model is called.

  3. Walk: breadth-first search

    A breadth-first search (BFS) visits all neighbours one step away, then all neighbours two steps away. It stops after depth 2. Each concept keeps its shortest hop (0, 1 or 2). There is no cap on the number of concepts.

  4. Collect and score

    Each concept points to its passages. A passage reached at two hops keeps the lower hop. A cross-encoder (CE) reads the question and one passage together and gives a relevance score. Our CE is bge-reranker-v2-m3.

  5. Rank: keep the top k_graph passages

    This is the bottleneck. Rule 7 sorts by hop, then by CE score, then by passage id. It keeps the first 10.

    Addendum D replaces rule 7 with hop-balanced/1. It gives the 10 slots to hops 0, 1 and 2 as 4 / 3 / 3. Each hop fills its slots with its best passages by CE score. Slots that stay free go to a backfill pass, and the chosen list is output in rule-7 order: hop, then descending CE score, then passage id.

  6. Fuse: reciprocal rank fusion

    The graph list (10) and the text list (dense top 30 plus BM25 top 30, CE on the top 10) are merged by reciprocal rank fusion (RRF): score = Σ 1 / (60 + rank). RRF reads ranks, not CE scores.

  7. Pack: the token budget B

    Passages go into the context in fused order while everything fits in B = 4,000 tokens. The first passage that does not fit stops the packing.

One worked example: the kernel-trick chain

Our KG has demo chains that need several steps. One chain is: kernel trick → Mercer kernel → positive definite matrix → eigenvalue.

For one demo question, the walk found 38 passages at hop 0, 174 at hop 1, 287 at hop 2 and 547 at hop 3. The thesis walk stops at hop 2, so 38 + 174 + 287 = 499 passages are candidates.

  1. Rule 7. Sort by hop first. Hop 0 alone has 38 passages, more than 10. So the 10 slots are all hop 0. The 461 deeper passages never reach fusion.
  2. What we saw. In 6 stored hybrid turns of the app, 120 of 120 graph-list items were hop 0.
  3. Balanced. Visit the hops in order. Each hop gets ⌈slots left ÷ hops left⌉ slots. Hop 0: ⌈10 ÷ 3⌉ = 4. Hop 1: ⌈6 ÷ 2⌉ = 3. Hop 2: ⌈3 ÷ 1⌉ = 3. Each hop gives its slots to its best passages by CE score.
  4. Short hops. A hop with too few passages passes its free slots on. Addendum D then adds a backfill pass: slots that are still free are filled in rule-7 order.
The problem was never "the walk is too short". The rank rule spent the whole budget on the nearest hop. Try it below.

    How other systems choose depth and share the budget

    A survey of graph RAG splits retrieval into once, iterative (fixed steps, or adaptive: the model decides when to stop) and multi-stage; ranking and pruning then cut what was retrieved. Our pipeline is once retrieval, non-adaptive, then CE pruning. The note groups 21 papers and one code base into three answers to "fixed or automatic depth?".

    How the depth is chosenWhat sets itExamplesLLM calls in retrieval
    1. Fixeda hyperparameter, set once on dev dataGRAG, LightRAG, GraphRAG local, RoG; our thesisnone to few (LightRAG: one, for keywords)
    2. A score instead of a depththe score of far nodes falls; no hop limitHippoRAG (PPR), PathRAG (decaying flow), G-Retriever (edge cost)none
    3. Decided while runninga model stops the walk, per questionToG sufficiency check, IRCoT, StructGPT, KAG, Self-Ask, Adaptive-RAG, FLARE, DRAGIN, Self-RAGmany; the depth changes per question

    Summary of the note, §3 . Details and marks for each system follow.

    1. Fixed depth plus a scoring rule

    • GraphRAG local search takes seed entities and their one-hop relations. It splits the context by fixed token proportions per source. Addendum D's hop quota belongs to this budget-split family.
    • LightRAG adds one-hop neighbours of the matched entities.
    • GRAG retrieves fixed 2-hop ego-graphs; 2 hops beat 1, and beyond 3 hops the embeddings oversmooth.
    • Think-on-Graph fixes width and depth at 3 as an upper limit, and its gains flatten past depth 3. Think-on-Graph 2.0 shows the same plateau and prunes with a BGE reranker.
    • Reasoning on Graphs plans relation paths with an LLM, then follows them with a constrained BFS.

    2. No depth: a soft score decides

    • HippoRAG runs Personalized PageRank (PPR) from the query nodes. A passage's score is the PageRank mass of its nodes, so far nodes get small scores without a hop limit. HippoRAG 2 also seeds PPR with the passage nodes.
    • PathRAG lets a resource flow decay by α = 0.7 per edge and cuts weak paths. In its ablation, the flow order beat a hop-first order.
    • G-Retriever picks a connected subgraph (a prize-collecting Steiner tree); an edge cost controls its size.

    3. A model decides when to stop

    • Think-on-Graph stops when the LLM judges the paths sufficient, at a cost of up to 2ND + D + 1 LLM calls.
    • IRCoT retrieves once per reasoning sentence and stops at "answer is". KG-GPT, StructGPT, KAG and Self-Ask also let the LLM split the question or call tools.
    • Adaptive-RAG decides before retrieval, with a small classifier. Self-RAG, FLARE and DRAGIN trigger retrieval from the model's own token signals.
    The common point. Every family has a ranking or budget step after the walk. None of them sorts "nearest hop first, then cut".

    Shares per source, or shares per hop

    Both GraphRAG local search and Addendum D split a fixed budget by fixed shares. They split along different lines.

    GraphRAG's shares stop one kind of evidence from filling the context. Our shares stop one hop from filling the graph list. Neither split is learned; each is a design choice.

    Why a BFS walk, and not PageRank or communities

    From the seeds, a graph algorithm decides what else is worth reading. Most GraphRAG systems use one of three. A k-hop walk (BFS) takes every neighbour up to k steps. Personalized PageRank (PPR) sends a random walker from the seeds that often jumps back to them; nodes where it spends time score high, and far, weakly linked nodes fade. Community detection (Leiden or Louvain) cuts the graph into dense topic groups at index time; an LLM writes a report per group, and the answer comes from the reports.

    Why not PPR, for now

    • Status. The thesis plans PPR as a later stage or an exploratory baseline.
    • Blind to edge labels. Weights per relation type would add a parameter to tune. H vs Hc would then test labels plus tuned weights.
    • A score, not a depth. It changes the candidates and the ranking together, so it is not like for like with the frozen depth 2.
    • A mixed record. HippoRAG is below ColBERTv2 on HotpotQA (R@5 77.7 vs 79.3). HippoRAG 2 leads the dense retriever NV-Embed-v2 by 59.8 to 57.0 average F1.

    Why not communities

    • Built for global questions. Our items ask about one concept and its prerequisites.
    • Cost. Our budget B is 4,000 tokens; one community-global query uses 353,889.
    • The gain shrinks under a fair budget. A token-matched RAG ties community-local search, 69.33 vs 69.01.
    • Status. No decision is recorded for retrieval. In the product, Louvain only colours the graph view.
    What the benchmarks cannot settle yet. No study we found compares PPR, Leiden communities and a k-hop BFS on one text-built graph under one token budget. Most comparisons let each system use its own budget, and LLM-judge verdicts flip when the answer order is swapped. A fixed B = 4,000 tokens across arms is the controlled design.
    Level 3our thesis choice

    Fixed depth, fixed quota, disclosed costs

    What we chose

    Depth 2 and the rank rule hop-balanced/1, frozen in protocol Addendum D before pilot 2.

    • Why a BFS walk. The thesis tests typed edges: H and Hc run the same BFS and differ only in the relation mask, while PPR or communities would add a second moving part (the comparison).
    • One rule, fixed before data. The confirmatory test compares H (the walk follows typed edges) with Hc (the same walk with the labels removed). An LLM or classifier that picks the depth per question adds a second moving part. It could differ between the arms and hide the label effect.
    • Common practice. ToG, GRAG, HippoRAG and PathRAG also fix their depth, width, damping or α as hyperparameters.
    • A known pattern. GraphRAG local search splits its context by fixed proportions per source; we split the graph list by fixed shares per hop.

    What it costs, and what it does not fix

    • Backfill can bring hop 0 back. With thin deeper hops the list can be 8 / 1 / 1. Each item reports its split.
    • Order still matters. The chosen passages stay in hop-first order, so fusion and packing can still push deep passages out of the 4,000 tokens. Models may also use the middle of a long context less well.
    • Compute. The balanced rule needs a CE score for every passage at hops 0-2, not only for hop 0.
    • Why we had to change. In pilot 1 as run, 29 of 30 graph lists were all hop 0: only 1 of 30 held a deeper-hop passage (measured). Under configuration I3 with rule 7 and the same seed concepts, none (0 of 30) was projected to hold one, so H and Hc would have sent identical contexts on all 30 items and the label test could not show anything. This is a projection, not an observation: it assumes that pilot 2 links the same seeds as pilot 1, and that assumption is not verified.
    • The router. Half the pilot questions (15 of 30) fall to the fallback row, where H = Hc.

    The product can try more

    Outside the thesis, the tutor app can test a soft score next: a hop-decay score (CE × γhop) or PPR from the seeds over the concept graph, both without LLM calls. An LLM-guided walk fits only an optional "deep explain" mode, because of its cost. For "overview" questions, pre-written summaries per lecture or per community, found by vector search, could stand in for full community map-reduce.
