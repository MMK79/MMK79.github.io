A layered lesson on a real pilot run

How a knowledge graph is built

From a lecture transcript to a graph that can answer "what must I learn first?", in six stages. We ran every stage on real data and kept every intermediate file. You can open each one below.

TL;DR

Six stages, one real run

  1. Chunks. The transcripts of Lectures 14 and 15 become passages, each with an id.
  2. Extraction. An LLM reads each passage and writes typed edges from a closed list of three relations. Every edge must quote its passage. It wrote mentions and kept edges.
  3. Entity resolution. Names that mean one thing ("SVM", "support vector machines") become one node. normalised names ended as concept nodes ( of them still candidates). An LLM review then rejected of the 30 merges the LLM judge proposed; no human checked either side.
  4. Entailment gate. Two small checkers score whether the passage supports each edge. of assertions pass. By our policy, "learn this first" edges are not decided by the checkers; they wait for a human (none reviewed them in this pilot).
  5. Graph files. All assertions, with their status and evidence, are written as plain CSV and JSONL files (the master copy). A Neo4j load script exists; it was not run.
  6. A question. "What must I understand before the kernel trick?" The strict walk over PREREQUISITE_OF edges finds nothing. A wider walk over USES finds one related concept, the kernel function: related, not a verified prerequisite.

LLM cost: US$ for the extraction and judge calls of the final run; US$ for all calls, trials and reruns included. The extraction and judge models were called through APIs (the course text went to those providers). The embedding model and the two checkers ran on free Kaggle machines, not on this computer.

Level 0in one minute

A detective's pinboard

For anyone. No technical words.

A detective has a box of witness statements. She wants one board that shows who knows whom.

1 · Cut

She cuts the statements into short pieces and numbers each piece.

2 · Pin

From each piece she writes cards for people and draws arrows: "Anna works for Bruno". Next to each arrow she pins the exact words.

3 · Merge

Two statements say "Bob, the driver" and "Robert Smith, the driver": the context shows one man, so one card. "Bob Junior" is another man with a similar name: two cards.

4 · Check

She rereads the words under each arrow and marks it: supported, unsure, or not supported. She keeps every mark. Her reading can be wrong too.

5 · Save

She photographs the board and writes the list of cards and arrows in her notebook.

6 · Ask

"Who does Anna work for, and who does that person work for?" She follows only the arrows that mean "works for".

In one line: a knowledge graph is a board of things and typed arrows. In our design every arrow also keeps the words it came from. Building one is cutting, pinning, merging, checking, saving and then asking.
Why the next idea: the detective does each step by hand. For a whole course, a program does them. Next, the same six steps on our course text, with the real output of each step.

Three ideas to keep

  1. A closed list of relations. The extractor may write only PREREQUISITE_OF, IS_A and USES. "A kernelised SVM uses a kernel function" is a fact about machine learning. "Dot product is a prerequisite of the kernel function" is a fact about learning: it says what to study first.
  2. Evidence on every edge. An edge without a passage id and a quote is not written. The quote shows where the edge came from; it does not prove the edge. The gate and a reviewer test that.
  3. Merge with care. The intended rule: two names merge only when they mean the same thing in this course. "Hard-margin SVM" is a kind of SVM, not the same, so it keeps its own node. An IS_A edge between them needs its own evidence.

The stages in the literature

For a researcher. Each claim has a source; the sources box at the end says which ones we read.

Extraction: open or schema-guided

Pretrained LLMs can extract relations with few-shot prompts and no task-specific fine-tuning (Wadhwa et al. 2023, per its abstract). Two families exist. Open extraction lets the model write any label; then a canonicalisation step must map the labels to a schema. EDC does this in three phases: extract openly, write a definition for each relation, then match the definitions to a target schema; without a target schema it builds one ("self-canonicalisation") (Zhang and Soh 2024, Sec. 3.1-3.2). Note: EDC canonicalises relations, not entity names. Schema-guided extraction gives the model the closed list from the start. We use schema-guided extraction because the thesis claim is about three relation types; a closed list keeps extraction and the experiment simpler to control (open extraction plus a fixed mapping would also be possible).

The GraphRAG indexer extracts entities and relations per chunk, merges entities that have the same name and writes summaries (Edge et al. 2024). It reports that smaller chunks gave more entity references than larger ones (600 vs 2,400 tokens, on its test data). That motivates short chunks; it does not show that our 1,600-character lecture chunks are best. LightRAG has the same three steps (recognise, profile, deduplicate) and adds new documents by taking the union (Guo et al. 2024).

Entity resolution

The problem is old: statistics calls it record linkage. Fellegi and Sunter's model has two thresholds; pairs between them go to clerical review (1969). Our score bands borrow that three-way pattern, without its statistical model. Blocking reduces the number of pairs to compare (Papadakis et al. 2020). On record-matching benchmarks, zero-shot GPT-4 with its best prompt matched about as well as fine-tuned matchers, and smaller models were much more sensitive to the prompt (Peeters et al. 2025, Tables 3-4). That is product and bibliographic records, not lecture concepts. Choosing among candidates worked better than yes/no pairs in record matching (Wang et al. 2025, abstract); this motivated our select-style judge, whose five candidates and six answers are our own untested choices. KGGen clusters names with an LLM and keeps the alias clusters as data (Mo et al. 2025, Sec. 4); we keep the alias mappings too, and add a merge log with the judge's reason and the review. In the merge code of Microsoft GraphRAG, LightRAG and nano-graphrag, entities merge only when the (upper-cased) name is the same (code read); that step does not reconcile "SVM" with "support vector machine" unless the extractor already wrote one name. Clusters are scored with B-cubed against a gold set (Bagga and Baldwin 1998).

The entailment gate

Small trained checkers can test whether a passage supports a claim. MiniCheck-Flan-T5-Large (770M parameters) reaches 74.7 balanced accuracy on LLM-AggreFact against 75.3 for GPT-4 (Tang et al. 2024, Table 2). HHEM-2.1-open is a smaller open checker; its numbers come from the vendor's card (Vectara). Two limits matter here. First, neither result says anything about spoken lecture captions; accuracy on our text must be measured, not assumed. Second, a passage that supports a claim does not prove that the claim came from it (Wallat et al. 2024).

Quality and questions

Quality surveys list accuracy, completeness and consistency as dimensions (Zaveri et al. 2015). For an LLM-built graph we plan to check them as schema conformance, rule checks (no cycles in PREREQUISITE_OF), duplicates, a completeness check against the syllabus, and a sample read by a person; this pilot ran only the rule and schema checks. Competency questions, which the finished graph should answer, help set its scope and the relations it needs (Grüninger and Fox 1995; Noy and McGuinness 2001).

Why the next idea: the literature gives the parts. The thesis has to choose among them and say what this pilot changes.
Level 3our thesis pipeline

What we build, and what this pilot taught us

For the thesis. Design from our notes (ontology v1, ER, NLI, batch pipeline); findings from this run.

The design

PartDecision
RelationsClosed list. The thesis claim C2 tests three: PREREQUISITE_OF (pedagogical), IS_A and USES (domain). DEFINES and MENTIONS link passages to concepts; HAS_LECTURE and COVERS come from the course structure, not from extraction.
EvidenceOne assertion per (source, relation, target); one or more evidence records, each with passage_id, passage_hash, quote, reading (stated or implied), extractor, checker scores. No evidence, no edge (rule R6).
Entity resolutionNormalise → alias table → candidates (rapidfuzz + bge-m3) → LLM judge on the uncertain band (KGGen-style clusters) → review queue → reversible merge log.
GateEach evidence record is scored: "supported" when both checkers give at least 0.5 to the claim against the whole chunk. An IS_A or USES assertion is accepted when at least one of its records is supported; review when the checkers disagree; rejected when all its records fail both. PREREQUISITE_OF: scored but, by policy, left for instructor review.
Stores (target)The master copy of the graph is plain files in git (done in this pilot); Neo4j is rebuilt from them (script written, not run). Passages, their text and their vectors go to Postgres + pgvector (not done here). Experiments are to read a hashed snapshot, never a live database.
ComputeEmbedding and checkers run on free machines (Kaggle here) with pinned code; course text uploaded there goes into private datasets that are deleted after download (checked by absence in this run). The LLM calls send the text to the API providers.

What the pilot measured

measured in this run
  • Alias matching alone linked of mentions to the seed vocabulary. The rest needed candidates and a judge.
  • The judge () proposed merges into vocabulary concepts and clusters. An LLM review (no human) read each sentence and rejected of these . Neither side was checked by a person. Rejections it made include: an adjective ("quadratic" → quadratic programming), an error estimate into a method ("validation error" → validation), and one answer whose reason named a different target than its choice.
  • The extractor labelled of edges "implied" and "stated". These are its own labels, not checked.
  • The funnel: kept edges → evidence records after entity resolution (edges to dropped names and loops removed) → distinct assertions → accepted by the gate.
  • Both checkers scored evidence records: HHEM put at 0.5 or above, MiniCheck only . Of the 319 IS_A and USES records, passed both, failed both and had different decisions; the other 71 (PREREQUISITE_OF) went to review whatever their scores.
  • The gate rejected "kernel trick USES dot product": its quote reads "It's not a straight inner product, but an inner product after a transformation". The fact is plausible (our reading). Why both checkers scored it low is not known; the negation in the sentence is one hypothesis. The gate estimates whether the chunk supports the claim; it is not a test of the world.
  • Rule checks on the non-rejected assertions found cycles ( in USES, in PREREQUISITE_OF, in IS_A) and USES assertions that break our rule "the source of USES is a method". The cause (loose extraction, wrong kinds, wrong merges, or a rule that is too narrow) needs a sample read.
  • No extracted edge says that something is a PREREQUISITE_OF the kernel trick, so the strict competency question has no answer.
what we conclude (our reading, not yet tested)
  • The judge should never merge without review in the thesis graph, or its "same" band needs a higher bar (both scores high, and the same kind). A gold set of mention pairs must measure this before the freeze.
  • The gate needs calibration on our own text: a threshold of 0.5 is the checkers' default, not a value tuned for lecture speech, and the two checkers disagree on many edges. The gold triples will give a precision-recall curve per relation and show which checker to trust.
  • Hypothesis: "learn this first" is seldom said in a lecture (here it may also be missed by extraction, chunk borders or captions). If it holds, prerequisite edges must come mostly from course structure, syllabi and human review; the plan is to freeze them by hash before the test questions are written, and to write those questions independently.
  • The strict v1 rule gives no answer. A walk that may also use gate-accepted USES returns the kernel function, but "uses" is not "must be learned first": it is a related concept, not a verified prerequisite. Whether such a rule belongs in the ontology is an open design question.

Not done in this pilot: a human gold set, a second rater, the Postgres load, the Neo4j load, the slides and the book. This is two lectures of eighteen.

Quiz

Five questions

1. Why does every extracted edge carry a quote from its passage?

The quote is provenance: it shows where the edge came from. Whether the passage supports the edge is tested separately, by the gate and by a reviewer (rule R6: no evidence, no edge).

2. The judge said "dimension" is the same as "VC dimension". The sentence was about the input dimension d. What is the right result?

A flag does not undo a false identity. When in doubt, keep apart; the log makes the decision visible and reversible.

3. In our design, why does a person, not the entailment gate, decide PREREQUISITE_OF edges?

It is a policy from our NLI note, not a measured limit: the checkers did score these edges here, but their scores were not used to accept or reject them.

4. Where is the master copy of the graph?

Files can be diffed and replayed; the plan is that experiments read a hashed snapshot, never a live database. (The Neo4j rebuild was not run in this pilot.)

5. Without edge types, two hops from the kernel trick reach many concepts. What is missing?

Types make relation-specific walks possible; they do not by themselves give a valid learning order. The thesis ablation will test the label effect with everything else held equal; this page only shows a demonstration.
