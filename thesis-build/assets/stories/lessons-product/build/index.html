Where a concept lives

The knowledge graph knows thousands of concepts. A learner needs to know where each one is taught: in which lecture, in which lesson. This page shows the rules that decide it, why the first rules failed, and the lessons and book companions those rules point to.

6,814 concepts · 464 covered by a lecture · 233 on the course map · 84 lessons · 12 companions in phase 1

How the graph itself was built is in Building the knowledge graph. This page starts where that one ends.

TL;DRthe short version

In six lines

  1. Two products place the same 6,814 concepts: the course map puts each under one lecture, the lessons give each one owning topic.
  2. The first rules counted mentions. Lecture 1, an overview, then owned SVM and backpropagation, and a 2-mention aside put PCA in lecture 4.
  3. The rule now in use weighs a definition 3 and a mention 1, needs weight 3, and lets a defining lecture beat a mentioning one: 233 of 464 covered concepts are placed.
  4. For lessons, a share over the whole corpus hid VC dimension from every topic. Now the share counts lecture video and only passages inside topics, plus a name match and a small alias table.
  5. 84 lessons are authored: video, interactive on real data, the idea in each book, practice and sources. The tutor shows them after a placement test and under each answer.
  6. Book companions are one page per chapter. The lessons and companions run in the local product; linking companions to the tutor is planned.
Level 0like I am five

Which shelf does the book go on?

A library has many shelves. Each book goes on one shelf, so you know where to look.

A cookbook says the word "garden" once. It does not go on the garden shelf. A book that explains gardens does.

Our tutor does the same with ideas. An idea goes to the lecture that explains it, not to the lecture that only says its name. If no lecture explains it well enough, it gets no shelf. That is better than the wrong shelf.

find every lecture that talks about the idea→explains it? 3 points→only names it? 1 point→most points wins, if 3 or more
Level 1student

Three rules, two failures, one worked example

A concept is a node in the knowledge graph (KG), such as "VC dimension". A passage is a piece of course text: a lecture transcript window, a slide or a book section. The KG records two links from a passage to a concept. DEFINES: the passage explains the concept. MENTIONS: the passage uses its name. A COVERS link says a lecture introduces or revisits a concept.

The course map in the Intelligent Educator shows one column per CS 156 lecture. Each covered concept must sit in exactly one column. The rule changed twice.

v1The first lecture that introduces the concept. Lecture 1 is an overview, so it took 61 of the 464 concepts, SVM and backpropagation among them.
v2At least 2 passages; the most passages win. PCA went to lecture 4 from a Q&A aside: "This is a different subject". Under v3 it has weight 2 there and leaves the map.
v3A definition weighs 3, a mention 1. Below 3, no claim. A lecture that defines beats one that only mentions. The earliest wins a tie. Result: 233 of 464 placed.

One real example, worked: noise

noise, a concept in the frozen KG

The counts. Lecture 4 defines noise once and mentions it 4 times: weight 3 + 4 = 7. Lecture 11 mentions it 21 times and never defines it: weight 21.

v2 says lecture 11. It has the most passages.

v3 says lecture 4. Both lectures pass the minimum of 3. Lecture 4 defines noise and lecture 11 only mentions it, so lecture 4 wins. Lecture 4, "Error and Noise", is where the course teaches it.

What it shows. Mentions pile up where a concept is used; definitions sit where it is taught.

The same trap in the lessons

A lesson belongs to a topic, one of 84. Each concept gets an owning topic, so the tutor can say "learn this lesson". The first rule: a topic owns a concept when its core book sections hold at least 34 % of all the concept's defining passages.

VC dimension has 19 defining passages: 5 in lectures, 11 in book sections that no topic covers, 6 in the vc-dimension topic. Six of 19 is under 34 %, so no topic owned it, and the VC-dimension lesson was never suggested.

    Level 2academic

    Evidence weighting, not a threshold on counts

    Both failures have one cause: a count that treats every occurrence as equal evidence. In the course map, mention counts track where a concept is used. In the lessons, a share over the whole corpus punished concepts whose evidence sits outside the curated sections. The fixes weigh definitional evidence above mentions, set a minimum support, and measure the share where the topics live.

    The lesson rules, stated exactly

    Any one of three rules gives ownership. (a) A topic's units include its lecture-video windows, and the share (at least 0.34) is taken over the defining passages that lie in some topic. (b) A concept whose label or alias fully matches a seed topic's title pattern belongs to it. (c) A 6-entry alias table covers names the KG spells differently: dot product, gradient, sigmoid, softmax, MAP estimation, hypothesis set. The ownership file adds an explicit override, which wins.

    The fix moved owned concepts from 1,565 to 1,821 or more, and learn-first links from 14 to 29 of 40 seed topics. The file in use today, after the topic list was rebuilt to 84 topics, lists 1,818 owned concepts. Broad concepts were left unowned on purpose. In today's file, generalization error still has no owner; the central limit theorem and partition function now belong to topics added after the course.

    Prerequisites need two votes

    A "learn first" link between two lessons needs at least 2 PREREQUISITE_OF edges in the KG, because that relation is still about 8 % wrong in a blind sample. A single edge shows as a lighter "see also".

    How good is the topic map?

    Topics come from a cross-book map: 2,828 sections of 9 books and the 18 CS 156 lectures. On 10 topics, precision is 0.81 strict and 0.98 lenient. Recall was not measured.

    Open

    The two products use different ownership rules for the same KG: a weighted lecture rule and a topic rule of share, name and alias. That is acceptable while they answer different questions; one shared, tested module would stop drift. For companions, script checks prove attribution and originality, not correctness: another model family found 26 problems in a draft that passed every script.

    Level 3our thesis choice

    Product features, kept outside the experiment

    These rules are design rules from the KG to the learner-facing views, for the method chapter. They do not touch the C2 experiment: its arms read the frozen KG directly. The lessons are written ahead of time, one per topic. A lesson written by an agent for one student's gap was a feasibility study only: researched, not built, and there is no plan to build it now.
