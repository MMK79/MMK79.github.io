Scale: 6 real ML courses, 4 universities, about 130 triples. The data is real; the topic typing and the T-box are our own modelling. Illustration, not an evaluation.

An interactive story in eight steps

Ontology, and what a knowledge graph does with it

One small knowledge base about real machine-learning courses runs through the whole page. You edit triples, run a reasoner, validate extractions and retrieve with it. Every number on this page is computed in your browser, and the reasoner is checked against Python (rdflib, owlrl, pyshacl).

Data: university ML-course crawl (universities/exports/site/courses.json and the cached syllabus pages). three.js is not used: the ideas are graphs and rules, and 2D SVG shows them well.

Step 1 · the picture

Taxonomy, ontology, knowledge graph: one glance

Your notes say: a taxonomy is a hierarchy, an ontology adds relations, a knowledge graph is the ontology applied to data. That is a good start. Press the three buttons and see what each one adds to the same data.

Why the next idea: "a graph of facts" needs a concrete data model. The standard one is RDF: everything is a triple.
Step 2 · the data model

RDF: everything is a triple

A triple is subject, predicate, object. Subjects and predicates are IRIs (global names). Objects are IRIs, literals (text, numbers) or blank nodes (things without a name, like one offering of a course). Add or remove triples; the graph follows.

Blue = IRI node, amber = literal, purple dashed = blank node. Click a node to highlight its triples in the table.

Why the next idea: some of these triples describe single courses, others describe what a "course" or "covers" means. Mixing them makes reasoning impossible to explain. So we split them into layers.
Step 3 · layers

T-box, R-box and A-box

The T-box states things about classes. The R-box states things about properties (roles). The A-box states facts about individuals. In RDF it is all one graph; the layer is a way of reading a triple. Move triples between lanes, then check yourself.

Why the next idea: the T-box and R-box are not decoration. A program can read them and derive new A-box triples. That program is a reasoner.
Step 4 · reasoning

A tiny RDFS + OWL-RL reasoner, with reasons

Rules in the reasoner: subclass, subproperty, domain, range, inverse, transitive, and a disjointness check. Press the button. Every new triple shows which rule made it and from which premises.

Why the next idea: the reasoner trusts every triple it gets. An LLM extractor does not always say true things. We need a check that says "this does not fit the schema".
Step 5 · validation

SHACL-like shapes check what an LLM extracted

Three candidate triples, as an extractor might read them from real syllabus sentences. Check each against the shapes and the T-box.

Open world versus closed world

OWL assumes the world is open: a missing triple is unknown. SHACL checks the data you have, as if it were closed: a missing triple can be a violation. Pick a course and delete its covers triples.

Why the next idea: with typed, checked data we can retrieve by meaning, not only by words.
