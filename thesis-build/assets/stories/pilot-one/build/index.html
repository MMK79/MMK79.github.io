A layered story about the first real run

Pilot 1 and what it changed

Before the main experiment, we ran the full harness on 30 items. It did its job: it found five design defects while no main-pool item had been answered. This page shows what failed, what each failure cost, and how the pipeline changed from I0 to I4.

30 items · 4 of 5 arms · 300 generations · 5 defects · 2 protocol addenda · config I2 → I4

The run's own results page is the Stage A pilot log. This page tells what came after it.

TL;DRthe short version

In six lines

  1. Pilot 1 ran 30 items through arms L, R, H and Hc: 300 generations, 0 failures. Arm G was still building.
  2. The first pilot was an implementation failure that changed the design, not evidence about the claim.
  3. The router was a placeholder, so H and its control Hc were the same system. Gate 3, as written, could never fire.
  4. The retrieval arms abstained on 135 of 270 generations; no cause is attributed. The closed-book arm got 28 of 30 right.
  5. Addendum C fixed the prompt, the router, T10, gate 3 and the dev set. Addendum D replaced the rank rule so deeper hops get slots.
  6. I4 now binds everything for pilot 2 and the main run. Pilot 2 is pending, with arm G.
Level 0like I am five

A dress rehearsal before the show

A theatre group plays the whole show once before the real audience comes. Nobody counts that night as the real show. It is there to find what breaks.

In our rehearsal, two actors who should play different parts said the same lines. The light for one scene could never switch on. Some actors said "I can't answer" even when the answer was in their script.

So we fixed the script and the stage. We wrote down every fix before the next rehearsal. Then nobody can say we changed the show after we saw the audience.

plan→rehearse→find what broke→write the fixes down→rehearse again
Level 1student

What pilot 1 was, and what it showed

A pilot is a small run of the real experiment. Our harness sends each question to each arm, a way of answering. L answers with no passages. R retrieves text passages. H adds passages found by walking the typed graph. Hc is H with the edge labels collapsed, the control for the labels.

The pilot used 30 items: 20 primary items (prerequisite and cross-concept questions, the ones the claim is about) and 10 secondary. Most were proofs. One answer model, Sonnet 5.5, answered; R, H and Hc answered each item three times. Two judges from another model family scored each answer, under a USD 1.00 cap. They spent USD 0.356.

One real example, worked

prml:ex2.51, a proof exercise

The task. Prove trigonometric identities from exp(iA). The gold record names the passages that hold the facts the proof needs.

What H saw. Both required gold passages were in H's context.

What H did. It replied NOT_IN_MATERIAL. The prompt said: abstain if the passages do not contain the answer. A proof is never written out in a passage, so the rule is unclear for this kind of item.

What it does not show. Gold presence lowered abstention only a little (62 % with no required gold in context, 38 % with all of it). So we do not say the prompt caused the abstentions.

      Level 2academic

      Amending a registration in the open

      The claim, its two contrasts (H − R, then H − Hc) and the fixed-sequence exact McNemar tests were registered before any data. A pilot is allowed to change code and configuration; it is not allowed to quietly rewrite rules. Addendum C states it plainly: it amends the registration; it does not preserve it, and each replaced rule is reported as a replacement. What it preserves: no main-pool outcome informs any choice, and every choice is hashed before the data it governs exist.

      The manipulation check that could not fire

      Gate 3 asks whether the router changes H's input often enough for H − Hc to mean anything. On 20 primary items, 0/20 differing items still gives an exact one-sided upper bound of 0.139, above the 0.10 threshold, so the gate could never trigger. The replacement runs on all 30 items, where 0/30 triggers and 1/30 (bound 0.149) does not, and also triggers if no primary item differs. It is evaluated once.

      The H − R contrast was weakly manipulated too: H's packed list equalled R's on 12 of 30 items.

      Selecting on dev without peeking

      The new prompt and the router were tuned on dev items only, with rules fixed in advance. The prompt selector read abstention, not accuracy, and used no judge. R was tuned first by gold-evidence recall (depth 30, 10 reranked); every router round tied, and the tie rule, not the data, decided. Dev needed gold, so 18 answerable pilot candidates left the main pool for dev.

      From hop 0 to a balanced list

      Rule 7 sorted graph passages by hop, then score, and cut at k_graph. A seed concept alone has dozens of passages, so hop 0 filled the slots. As run, 1 of 30 graph lists held a deeper passage. Under I3 none would: a projection, not an observation. Addendum D gives each hop a quota, backfills unused slots in the old order and returns the set in that order. It reads no label. Rule 8 becomes an equal cap, not an equal length. An equal cap is not equal exposure: used tokens and list lengths differ by arm and are reported.

      Threats that stay open

      Cross-encoder truncation: 44 of 50 sampled pairs reach the 512-token cap. Fusion: RRF can let R's list take over the context; we report this two-list dominance per item. Router: 15 of 30 pilot questions take the default row, where H and Hc allow the same edges. Labels: fourteen rules close the channels in our pipeline through which labels could reach Hc, with residual channels (topology, node text, graph construction) still open. Strata: kappa 0.33 on the exact stratum, 0.20 on primary membership.

      Judge agreement in pilot 1: same verdict 153 of 165, kappa 0.77. Descriptive McNemar on 30 items: H − R b = 4, c = 2, p = 0.344.

      Level 3our thesis choice

      What stays, what goes, what is next

      The fixes touch configuration, not data. The graph, the passages, the gold evidence, the strata, the bank and the reference answers all stay valid; I2 is replaced. Pilot-1 answers stay as a descriptive record and a historical comparator, never as evidence for the claim.

      The claim, scoped

      Closed-book, the model already knew 28 of 30 answers. So the claim now says what it tests: which retrieval gives a tutor restricted to the course material more judged-correct answers, not whether retrieval adds knowledge. L is reported beside every arm. A gold-citation proxy runs as a secondary test only.

      Pilot 2 and arm G

      Pilot 2 reruns the same 30 items under I4 with all five arms. It is an exposed development rerun: its estimates are descriptive, and no outcome-driven change may follow it. L answers closed-book three times; G answers once. Arm G is pending: its first full build stopped at a 12-hour limit, and a clean staged rebuild is running.

      Results of pilot 2 are sealed until the pilot closes. The scoring behind the balanced rule is in One scorer, set before tuning; the rule itself is in Graph hops and ranking; the new answer prompt is in Choosing the answer prompt.
