A layered lesson on the second stage of search

Rerankers

A fast search finds candidates. A slower, careful model puts them in a better order. Why two models, which one we run, where it runs, and what the benchmarks do and do not say.

Builds on Indexing for RAG (how the fast search works) and links to What happens when a student asks (the whole question path).

TL;DRthe short version

In five lines

  • A reranker is a second-stage model. It takes a short list from a fast search and puts it in a better order. It reads the question and one passage together and gives one score: that design is a cross-encoder.
  • The fast search uses a bi-encoder (bge-m3): one vector per text, made before the texts meet. Fast over all chunks, but it sees topic, not "does this answer the question".
  • We run bge-reranker-v2-m3 (568M parameters, Apache-2.0, multilingual) as int8 ONNX on CPU, in three places: the text list R (top 10), the graph list (order inside each hop group) and the "should I abstain?" gate.
  • Its benchmark numbers are the vendor's own (model card). Another vendor's run shows a case where it made the order worse. Our own test on the course questions is still to run.
  • The recorded reasons for the choice are practical (inherited code, own compute, cost), not a benchmark.
Level 0like I am five

A fast finder and a careful reader

Imagine a library with 10,000 pages. You ask a question.

First, a fast finder looks at every page in a blink. It gives you the 50 pages that look related.

Then a careful reader reads your question next to each of those 50 pages, one at a time. For each page it asks: "Does this page answer this question?" It sorts the 50 pages by the answer.

The careful reader is the reranker. It is too slow to read 10,000 pages, but 50 is fine. Its job is order, not search. It can only move up a page that the fast finder already found.

all pages→fast finder→short list→careful reader→better order
Level 1student

Two ways to compare a question and a passage

A bi-encoder turns the question into one vector and each passage into one vector. A vector is a list of numbers that stands for the meaning of a text. Relevance is the cosine of the two vectors. The passage vectors are made once, when we build the index. So the search is fast.

A cross-encoder joins the question and one passage into one input. The model reads both and gives one number: the relevance of this pair. It must run again for every question and every passage. So it is slow. It is more accurate because every word of the question can look at every word of the passage. In a bi-encoder the two texts never meet before the comparison.

The raw output is a logit (any real number). The model card and our backend change it to a value from 0 to 1 with a sigmoid. This page shows that 0-1 value.

      Level 2academic

      Evidence, benchmarks and cost

      The longer treatment of first-stage indexes, BM25, hybrid fusion and the cross-encoder's place among them is Indexing for RAG, Level 2. This section adds what that page does not cover.

      Why rerank after dense retrieval

      Nogueira and Cho reranked BM25 results with BERT on MS MARCO and reached the top of the leaderboard, 27% (relative) above the previous state of the art in MRR@10 . MRR@10 went from 16.7 to 36.5 ; the research note did not re-read this table, the Indexing lesson did. Across the 18 zero-shot datasets of BEIR, "re-ranking and late-interaction-based models on average achieve the best zero-shot performances, however, at high computational costs" .

      When it does not help

      A reranker is only as good as its training data. In the Qwen3-Reranker card every reranker reorders the top 100 from Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B. On English MTEB-R the first stage alone scores 61.82 and bge-reranker-v2-m3 after it 57.03: the order got worse. On Chinese CMTEB-R it helped slightly, 71.02 to 72.16 . "Always add a reranker" is therefore not a law; it must be measured on our questions . The thesis does this: rerank_top was tuned on the dev split (C4) .

      What it costs

      • Compute grows with the list: cost = number of candidates × one full model pass. A list of 10 is cheap; a list of 600 is not.
      • Measured: about 0.5 pairs/s on a Kaggle 4-CPU kernel, int8 ONNX, one pair at a time; run rerank-0 scored 1,477 pairs in 2,841 s . In the product the graph arm scored 627 passages in 7-8 min before the cap . Memory about 0.9 GB .
      • Truncation: our code cuts each pair to 512 tokens; the vendor evaluation used 1,024 . Long passages lose their tail .
      • int8 results can depend on the CPU instruction set, so the thesis pins the scoring job .
      Level 3our thesis choice

      Where it runs and why this model

      The tutor answers questions about one course (CS 156 lectures) and must cite its sources. The thesis compares a text arm R with graph arms such as H; the same reranker serves both.

      We run BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3 as onnx/model_int8.onnx from an automatic ONNX conversion, one pair at a time, loaded on first use . The thesis arm R is "BM25 + pgvector BGE-M3, RRF k = 60, cross-encoder rerank, tuned on dev" . The plan was the same int8 file on Kaggle CPU (S1), with an fp32 GPU re-score as the fallback (S2) . S1 was kept: every Kaggle kernel (25/25, on Intel and AMD CPUs) gave bit-equal scores on the 200-key overlap check, so S2 did not run; the merged file holds 197,073 scores . How the scores were made: Scoring Sources.

      question→BM25 + dense → RRF→rerank top 10·graph walk→order in each hop→pack→G1 gate
      1 · text list R

      Reorder the top 10 of the fused list

      BM25 and dense lists are fused by RRF (k = 60). The cross-encoder scores the first rerank_top = 10 candidates, sorts them by score (then id) and puts them before the rest. rerank_top = 0 turns the reranker off everywhere . The step-by-step path with real scores: Query Flow, step through.

      2 · graph list

      Order passages inside each hop group

      The concept walk returns passages with a hop number. The thesis rule for H and Hc is hop-balanced/1: the slots are shared across hops 0, 1 and 2 (4 / 3 / 3 at 10 slots), each hop takes its best passages by cross-encoder score, free slots are backfilled, and the list is output by hop, then descending score, then id . The earlier rule 7 sorted the whole list by hop, then score, then id, so hop 0 took every slot; it is kept only as history. The balanced rule of Addendum D needs a score for every hop group and refuses to run without the reranker . In the product only, a hop group above 60 passages has only its 60 closest (by dense score) scored; the thesis arms have no cap .

      3 · G1 gate

      Should the tutor say "I don't know"?

      The highest reranker score among the packed passages is compared with 0.05; below it, the tutor abstains. Without the reranker the gate uses the best dense cosine (0.45). Both thresholds are placeholders, not calibrated . The gate with live scores: Query Flow, "I don't know".

      An older chat path recalls 20 candidates, reranks and keeps 5; on a reranker failure it falls back to the recall order .

      Why this model: what is recorded

      Inherited and already wired; the partner's HTTP reranker was unreachable and was removed under "own APIs only"; a Cohere fee would have been the largest line ($15) of the offline evaluation, while our own compute costs nothing per query; kept optional until it passed a latency and memory gate on the Mac (the backend now fits in 2.05 GB with encoder and reranker); same family as our bge-m3 encoder .

      Not found in any record
      • Chosen because it is multilingual and covers Persian. True as a property (MIRACL Persian 62.49 on the card), not found as a reason.
      • Chosen because of its size and the int8 CPU export.
      • Compared with jina, mxbai or Qwen3 rerankers first. No comparison found.
      • A benchmark (BEIR or MIRACL) drove the choice.
      Reading and next stepsA defensible default: permissive licence, multilingual with a Persian number, CPU-friendly, same family as the encoder. It is no longer the strongest at its size. For the thesis this matters little, because R and H share the same reranker . So: keep it frozen for the thesis and report the model, revision, int8 and 512-token truncation; test Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B on our dev questions for the product; calibrate the G1 gate on human-labelled dev items; run a small Persian check before Persian content goes live .

      Not measured yet: whether reranking helps on our own course questions (R with rerank_top = 0 vs 10 on dev), how much the 512-token cut removes, and whether the product's 60-passage cap changes which graph passages are packed.
