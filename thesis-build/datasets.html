The datasets the tutor is tested on

Before the tutor meets students, it is tested offline: the same questions go to a plain chatbot, vector search (RAG), the course knowledge graph (GraphRAG) and a hybrid. This page lists every question set and graph we checked, which five we use, and how we build our own. Links were checked on 2026-10-02.

Names, figures and source text on this page are kept in English, as published; the headings and labels are in Persian.

The five we use

No public question set is tied to a machine-learning course's own material, so our own bank is the main test and the rest anchor and check it.

    Big graphs of computer-science research

    If the big CS graph with an API means a citation graph, it is OpenAlex (15.5 million computer-science works, open REST API, CC0) or DBLP (computer science only, SPARQL). If it means a knowledge graph extracted from papers, it is CS-KG 2.0 (14.5 million papers, 67.5 million statements). ORKG has an API and a QA benchmark but holds about 69,000 papers. None is a course-level concept graph, so none replaces our own; they serve as vocabulary and alignment targets.

    Our own question bank

    300–500 questions, each checked by a person against the cited pages, labelled by type. Target shares:

    • Factual · 20%A definition, formula or property found in one passage.
    • Conceptual · 25%Why, compare, or when to use, from one or two passages.
    • Multi-step over prerequisites · 25%Needs concept A to explain B: two or three steps along the concept graph.
    • Procedural · 15%Derive, compute, or the steps of an algorithm.
    • Misconception · 10%The student states a wrong premise and the tutor has to correct it.
    • Out of scope · 5%Not in the course; the right answer is to say so.

    Questions written from a graph path favour GraphRAG and questions written from one passage favour vector search, so we write them both ways and report each route separately. A quarter of the items are double-checked by a second person, and 20% are set aside before any approach is run.

          Lecture videos and courses we could learn from

          Free lecture playlists from universities, summer schools and conferences worldwide, plus the big course platforms and company academies. Playlists were counted from their metadata; no video was downloaded.

          Free and reusable: an open licence (Creative Commons, MIT OCW, open course repositories), so transcripts can go into the tutor's data. Free to watch: public, but the licence or terms do not allow reuse, so the tutor links to it. Paywalled: listed for completeness. Coursera and edX forbid scraping and AI training, so they are link-only.
