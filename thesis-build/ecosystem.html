Commitments by governments, big tech and philanthropy

Do not add these up. Many pledges overlap (the same money announced by a company and by a programme it funds), many are in kind (product licences, cloud credits) and valued by the donor, and an executive order or curriculum mandate may carry no new money at all.

AI products in higher education, computer science first

It starts with Harvard's CS50 suite (Liu et al., Teaching CS50 with AI, SIGCSE 2024), then the research tools listed in the computing-education reviews (Prather et al. 2025, Tabarsi et al. 2025), then commercial tools. In those reviews, tools with instructor-set guardrails reported positive results in 73% of studies (19 of 26) when students had no guidance, against 55% (12 of 22) for general chatbots. Most of that evidence is students' own ratings.
