An AI-based interactive approach to improving educational productivity

Automatic knowledge-graph construction and graph retrieval-augmented tutoring: a machine learning course case study.

Masoud Mohararzadeh KordMSc student, Industrial Engineering (402679063)
Prof. Mir Saman PishvaeeSupervisor
Prof. Behrouz MinaeiAdvisor

The approved title is the Persian one above. The claim, questions, hypotheses and arms below are the working design. They are not yet signed off by the supervisors.

The claim

On a locked set of held-out questions about one machine-learning course (Caltech CS 156) that are assigned to the prerequisite or cross-concept strata, we test whether hybrid retrieval over a course knowledge graph with three typed concept-to-concept relations (one pedagogical, PREREQUISITE_OF; two domain, IS_A and USES) gives a higher fully-correct rate than a tuned text retriever (BM25 and dense retrieval, fused and reranked) under the same context-token cap, and whether it gives a higher fully-correct rate than the same retrieval code on the same graph with the three labels collapsed to one; the second contrast estimates the incremental value of explicit relation labels at retrieval time in this implementation.

This is a pre-registered test, not a result. Both contrasts are tested as specified whatever the final sample size. The thesis states whether the power target of 270 primary items was reached; below that target, a non-rejection is reported as inconclusive and not as evidence of no effect. A null or negative result is a finding.

Research questions

  1. Efficacy. On the primary stratum, does the typed hybrid H give a higher fully-correct rate than the text retriever R under the common context-token cap?
  2. Mechanism. On the primary stratum, does H give a higher fully-correct rate than H-collapsed, which differs from H only in the relation-eligibility step of the traversal? On what share of items do the two arms send different inputs to the answer model?
  3. Strata (descriptive). How do the estimates of H − R and H − Hc vary across the assigned strata (prerequisite, cross-concept, definition, example, lookup, and abstention on unanswerable items)? Each stratum is reported separately with intervals; no interaction is tested.
  4. Cost. What is the cost per fully correct answer of each arm as a function of the share of primary-stratum questions and the number of questions served, including the amortised cost of building the graph, and in which region, if any, is H cheaper per correct answer than R?
  5. Graph quality. What are the precision and recall of each of the three relations against exhaustively annotated gold passages, and how does the fully-correct rate of H change when 25 % and 50 % of the edges are removed at random (exploratory)?

Hypotheses and decision

  1. Efficacy. H gives a higher fully-correct rate than R. Exact one-sided McNemar test, tested first.
  2. Mechanism. H gives a higher fully-correct rate than Hc. Tested only if H1 is rejected (fixed sequence); the estimate and its interval are reported in every case.
  3. Descriptive. On the lookup and definition strata, H − R is expected to be close to zero. Estimates and intervals only; no equivalence claim.
  4. Cost. Cost per correct answer of H and R is reported with the break-even region, including the result "no crossover".
  5. Exploratory. Robustness of H to random loss of edges, reported as a curve. No trend is asserted.

The claim holds only if H1 and H2 are both rejected. If H1 is rejected and H2 is not, the thesis states that the graph helps under this cap and that the labels add no detectable gain.

The arms being compared

Same answer model, same prompt, temperature 0 and the same context-token cap in every arm. Only the way it finds course knowledge changes.

L: plain LLMNo course material (closed book). The status quo students already use.
R: tuned text retrievalBM25 and dense retrieval, fused and reranked over the course text, tuned on a development set.
G: LightRAG baselineA published graph-RAG system, run with its defaults.
H: typed hybridWalks the course knowledge graph along typed relations and fuses the result with R. The thesis system.
Hc: H-collapsedThe same code on the same graph with the three labels collapsed to one. It shows what explicit labels add at retrieval time.
