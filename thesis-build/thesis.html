An AI-based interactive approach to improving educational productivity

Automatic knowledge-graph construction and graph retrieval-augmented tutoring: a machine learning course case study.

The approved title is the Persian one above. The questions, hypotheses and design below are the working design from the literature review. They are not yet signed off by the supervisors.

Research questions

  1. Construction. How accurately, completely and consistently can an LLM pipeline build a knowledge graph of an ML course (concepts, definitions, typed relations, prerequisites, provenance), compared with expert annotation?
  2. Answer quality. Compared with a plain LLM and vector retrieval, does graph retrieval improve correctness, faithfulness and hallucination rate on students' questions, and does the effect depend on the question type?
  3. Quality to usefulness. How much does answer quality drop when the graph is damaged (prerequisite edges removed, wrong triples added, concepts split)?
  4. Learning and productivity. Do students learn more, and more per hour of study, with the graph tutor than with vector retrieval or a plain LLM?
  5. Managerial. Under what mix of questions, cost and instructor effort is graph retrieval, or a router between methods, the cost-effective choice?

Hypotheses

  1. The LLM-built graph reaches concept precision ≥ 0.85, triple precision ≥ 0.75 and prerequisite-edge F1 ≥ 0.65 against expert gold. These are targets, not measurements.
  2. On relational, prerequisite and multi-hop questions graph retrieval is more correct than vector retrieval; on factual questions it is no worse within a pre-registered margin; both retrieval tutors hallucinate less than the plain LLM.
  3. Correctness on relational questions falls steadily as graph edges are removed or noise is added; removing prerequisite edges alone causes a significant drop.
  4. Normalised learning gain on an AI-free post-test is highest with the graph tutor, then vector retrieval, then the plain LLM, and gain per study hour follows the same order.
  5. A router (vector retrieval for simple questions, graph retrieval for complex ones) matches graph retrieval's correctness at half its cost per question or less.

The tutors being compared

Same language model, same prompt and the same "give hints, never full solutions to graded work" rule in every variant. Only the way it finds course knowledge changes.

Plain LLMNo course material. The status quo students already use.
Hybrid text retrievalKeyword search plus embeddings, fused and re-ranked over the course text.
Graph retrievalWalks a typed course knowledge graph (prerequisites, examples, parts). The thesis system.
LightRAGA published graph-RAG baseline, run unchanged.
RouterSends each question to the cheaper method that should answer it well.
Whole course in contextCache-augmented generation: the course is small enough to load in full, with no retrieval.
Agentic tutorAn agent that chooses between the same tools step by step, with a step cap.

How the study runs

  1. Month 1
    Secure the course corpus and permission; annotation protocol; seed vocabulary from the syllabus.
  2. Month 2
    Graph pipeline and its evaluation against expert annotation, with instructor review (RQ1).
  3. Month 3
    Baseline tutors, the question bank and the validated LLM judge.
  4. Month 4
    Graph tutor, ablations and graph-damage tests, cost logging (RQ2, RQ3, H5).
  5. Month 5
    Pilot with about five students, then the crossover study with pre- and post-tests (RQ4).
  6. Month 6
    Analysis, the productivity and managerial chapter, writing.
